Detecting driver drowsiness with computer vision

A tired driver's face changes before the car does. Eyes stay closed longer, yawns come more often, and the head starts to nod. This tutorial shows how a camera and a few well-chosen measurements can catch those signs in real time.

What this tutorial covers

Driver drowsiness is a major road-safety problem because fatigue slows a driver's attention and reaction time. This tutorial explores how computer vision can detect it by analyzing the driver's face in camera video. It focuses on visual signs such as eye closure, blinking patterns, yawning, and head movement, and on the techniques used to measure them, including facial landmark detection, the Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR), and PERCLOS.

It also compares traditional computer vision, machine learning, and deep learning approaches, and looks honestly at where existing systems succeed and fail: poor lighting, sunglasses, unusual camera angles, differences between drivers, and false alarms.
