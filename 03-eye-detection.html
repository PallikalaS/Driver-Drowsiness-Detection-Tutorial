Eye-Based Drowsiness Detection

The eyes are the strongest single signal of drowsiness. This page explains the two most widely used measurements.

Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR)

Explain each landmark p1 to p6, walk through the formula below, and give typical values (open eye about 0.25 to 0.30, closed below 0.20). Link to the animated eye on the home page.

EAR = ( ‖p2 − p6‖ + ‖p3 − p5‖ ) / ( 2 · ‖p1 − p4‖ )

Explain why the denominator is weighted by 2, and why a ratio stays stable when the driver moves closer to or farther from the camera.

Detecting blinks and prolonged eye closure

Explain the consecutive-frame rule: a blink drops EAR for about 100 to 400 ms, while drowsiness keeps it low much longer. Show how a frame threshold separates the two.

PERCLOS

PERCLOS = (frames with eyes ≥ 80% closed) / (total frames in window) × 100%

Explain the P80 definition, typical window lengths (about 1 minute), and the alert thresholds used in the literature [1]. Compare EAR (instant, per frame) with PERCLOS (summary over time).
